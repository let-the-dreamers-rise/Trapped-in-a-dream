# GATE CS — Final 30 Days Protocol (D61 → Exam Day)

Day numbering assumes a 90-day countdown ending on exam day (D90). D61 = 30 days out.
GATE CS runs in two slots: **Forenoon 09:30–12:30** and **Afternoon 14:30–17:30**. Everything below is anchored to your allotted slot.

**The one rule that governs all bands: no new topics after D61.** Toppers converge on this — new material in the last month creates confusion and stress, and displaces the revision that actually moves marks.

---

## Day-Band Table

| Band | Days out | DO | DO NOT |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D61–D75** (Consolidation) | 30 → 16 | Full-length mock every **2nd day** (~7–8 mocks in band), each at your exact slot time. Spend **2× the mock duration on analysis** — every wrong/skipped/guessed question goes into the error log with a root cause tag (concept gap / silly / misread / time panic). Second full syllabus revision using **your own short notes only**. Daily 30-min formula sheet drill. Re-solve PYQ (2010–2025) subject-wise for the 4 highest-weightage areas: Algorithms+DS, TOC+Compilers, OS, DBMS+Networks. Keep Engineering Maths + Aptitude (~28 marks combined) in daily rotation — 20 questions/day. | Do NOT start any new subject, new book, new YouTube playlist, or new coaching module. Do NOT attempt "left-over" topics from scratch — accept the leak. Do NOT do untimed practice; everything gets a clock. Do NOT chase an obscure topic because a Telegram group hyped it. Do NOT compare mock ranks with peers. |
| **D76–D85** (Sharpening) | 15 → 6 | Mock cadence drops to **every 3rd day** (3 mocks max in band) — you are past the point where more mocks add information. Between mocks: revise **only the error log** and the compressed artifacts. Build/finish the three final artifacts (below). Third pass over formula sheet + standard results (asymptotics, TOC closure properties, normal-form rules, subnetting, page-table arithmetic, pipeline/CPI formulas). One dedicated Aptitude+Maths session daily (30–40 min). Begin the **sleep shift**: move wake time 20–30 min/day until you are naturally awake and alert 3 hours before your slot start. | Do NOT binge mocks daily hoping for a score jump — score variance in this band is noise, not signal. Do NOT re-read full textbooks or standard notes cover-to-cover. Do NOT panic-download new test series. Do NOT solve very-hard "GATE-impossible" question sets — they wreck confidence for zero marks. Do NOT change your calculator, browser, or attempt-order strategy after this point. |
| **D86–D88** (Taper) | 4 → 2 | Revision artifacts **only**. Two half-length (90-min) sectional papers *at most*, purely to keep timing muscle warm. Daily full read of: formula sheet, one-liner sheet, error-log summary. Rehearse your **attempt strategy** on paper: pass 1 = all 1-mark + instantly-visible 2-mark (~60 min), pass 2 = the marked 2-mark questions (~90 min), pass 3 = review NAT answers, verify no MSQ half-guesses (~30 min). Verify admit card printout, photo ID, exam-centre route and travel time (do a dry run if the centre is unfamiliar). Sleep and wake exactly at exam-slot rhythm. | Do NOT take a full 3-hour mock — a bad score here is pure damage with no time to fix it. Do NOT learn a single new formula. Do NOT stay up past your target bedtime "finishing one more subject". Do NOT try new food, new gym intensity, or anything that risks illness. |
| **D89** (Day before) | 1 | Light skim of formula sheet + one-liners in the **morning only** (2–3 hours max). Pack the bag: admit card, original photo ID, pen, mask/whatever the brochure requires. Confirm reporting time (typically ~1.5–2 h before slot start; gates close well before the exam). Light physical activity, a walk, normal food. Screens off by early evening. In bed at your normal target time — 7–8 h sleep. | Do NOT study after evening. Do NOT open a mock, a test series, or the error log for "one last check". Do NOT discuss syllabus, cut-offs, or "did you cover X?" with anyone. Do NOT experiment with sleep aids or extra caffeine. Do NOT expect to sleep perfectly — one mediocre night does not cost you marks; anxiety about it does. |
| **Exam day** (D90) | 0 | Wake ~3 h before slot start. Normal breakfast — nothing new, nothing heavy. Glance at the formula sheet for 20–30 min max, then close it. Reach the centre early per the admit card's reporting time. In the hall: read instructions, note the virtual calculator and marking scheme (1/2 marks; **NAT and MSQ carry no negative marking**, MCQs do). Execute the three-pass plan. Time-box: never let a single question exceed ~3 min on pass 1. Mark-and-move ruthlessly. Attempt every NAT and MSQ you have any grounded basis for; on MCQs, guess only after eliminating at least two options. Keep 10 min at the end to sweep unanswered NATs. | Do NOT do any fresh problem-solving the morning of. Do NOT carry notes into the queue and read them anxiously. Do NOT anchor on the first hard question — it is a trap for exactly this. Do NOT recompute a completed section from scratch. Do NOT tally your score mid-exam or estimate rank between passes. Do NOT discuss the paper with anyone if you have a second shift/other exam ahead. |

## The Three Revision Artifacts (must exist by D85)

1. **Formula & standard-results sheet** — 4–6 pages, handwritten, subject-blocked. Asymptotic complexities table, recurrence master theorem cases, TOC closure/decidability grid, normalization rules, ACID/isolation table, page-replacement and address-translation arithmetic, subnet/CIDR math, TCP window formulas, CPI/pipeline/speedup, combinatorics + probability standard results.
2. **One-liner / trap sheet** — every conceptual gotcha you have ever got wrong once: "LALR vs SLR conflict", "is this language regular but not DCFL?", "does this schedule allow view-serializability?", "off-by-one in B+ tree order". One line each, no derivations.
3. **Error log summary** — your mock mistakes collapsed into ~20 recurring failure modes with the fix next to each. This is the single highest-value document you own in the last two weeks.

| D89–D90 | 0 | — | Protect confidence |

## Sleep Shift Protocol

- **Forenoon slot (09:30):** by D85 you should wake ~06:15–06:30 daily, and have done at least three mocks starting at 09:30 sharp. Your brain must have peaked at 09:30 before, not for the first time on exam day.
- **Afternoon slot (14:30):** do not sleep in. Wake at a normal morning hour, keep a light lunch, and schedule every mock at 14:30. Guard against the post-lunch dip by rehearsing exactly the meal you plan to eat on exam day.
- Move wake time by no more than 20–30 min per day; abrupt shifts cost more than they gain.
- Target 7–8 h nightly from D76 onward. Sleep debt in the last two weeks measurably degrades the working memory GATE leans on.

## What Actually Changes Your Score in 30 Days

Ranked by return on the time invested:
1. Cutting silly mistakes (usually 5–12 marks, and the cheapest to fix via the error log).
2. Attempt strategy and time allocation — leaving 8 solvable questions untouched is a bigger loss than any weak topic.
3. Aptitude + Engineering Maths (~28 marks, disproportionately under-revised, highly scoreable).
4. Formula recall speed on high-weightage subjects.
5. Deepening an already-strong topic. *Not* on this list: learning a new weak topic from zero.
